# Physik — subject guide

Subject-wide teaching guidance for Physik. Class-specific observations belong in
the class's own memory pages, not here.

## Core planning principles

- Open on a phenomenon or measurement students can argue about; introduce the
  model as the thing that explains it, not as the thing to memorise.
- Name the model's limits out loud. "This works until…" is physics content.
- Keep mathematics in service of the physics: students should be able to say
  what an equation claims before they rearrange it.
- Every practical carries a prediction and a reconciliation — predict, measure,
  explain the gap.

## Typical misconceptions (Gymnasium Physik)

- Current is "used up" around a circuit rather than conserved.
- Force is needed to sustain motion rather than to change it.
- Heat and temperature are treated as the same quantity.
- Energy is "lost" rather than transferred to a less useful form.
- Weight and mass are used interchangeably.

## Reusable question patterns

- "What would you see if <model> were wrong here?"
- "Which quantity are we actually measuring, and with what uncertainty?"
- "Where does the energy come from, and where does it end up?"

## Safety and feasibility

Low-voltage supplies and prepared kits keep attention on the reasoning. Check
apparatus count against group size before planning a practical; a demo with a
strong prediction step beats a rushed class experiment.
