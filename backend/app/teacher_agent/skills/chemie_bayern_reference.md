# Bavaria Chemistry - Gymnasium Grade 9 NTG

This reference is mandatory for Chemistry 9 NTG lesson production. It combines
the active class's inherited framework profile with Chemistry pedagogy. Use the
profile for class-effective principles and trusted sources for official claims.

## Route and ground

Confirm the class route as Bavaria, Gymnasium, Chemistry, Grade 9, NTG. Before
naming an official competency, progression, or requirement, search and read the
relevant trusted-source section. Do not infer what the class has learned from a
curriculum document; use lesson history and class memory for that.

## Investigation before explanation

Begin with an observable chemical phenomenon, a data problem, or a concrete
substance-level question when feasible. Students first generate observations,
measurements, comparisons, or arguments. Formal terminology and explanation
follow the evidence rather than replacing it.

## Chemistry representations

Connect substance-level observations, particle models, and symbolic language
(formulae, equations, variables, or quantitative relations) when appropriate.
Name the purpose of each representation and make transitions explicit. A
particle drawing is a model only when students use it to explain evidence and
revise it after learning.

## Learning goals and evidence

State a small number of observable goals across content understanding,
scientific practice, and application/meaning. For each goal identify the
prerequisite, the evidence students will produce, and an observable look-for or
exit check.

## Anticipate ideas and respond

Record up to three likely student ideas in this exact teacher-facing form:

**What / why / teacher move**

- What students may think or do.
- Why the idea is plausible or persistent.
- The question, representation, task change, or discussion move that tests it.

## Lesson sequence

For a typical 45-minute lesson, use a realistic sequence: launch/context,
evidence task or investigation, sensemaking/argumentation, model or symbolic
explanation, and formative exit evidence. Adapt the sequence to available
materials and actual class history; do not add a practical activity without a
safety boundary and a meaningful evidence purpose.

## Differentiation and inclusion

Preserve the common Chemistry question and evidence task. Provide multiple
access routes through vocabulary, particle-model scaffolds, data displays,
structured roles, and fading prompts. Keep student-facing material in English
for this build; retain German source labels only where fidelity requires them.

## Non-negotiables

- Investigation before explanation when an evidence task is feasible.
- Model revision rather than decorative diagrams.
- Explicit safety, material, and timing realism.
- Look-fors linked to student evidence, not generic engagement.
- Exit evidence that tests transfer or changed reasoning, not recall alone.
