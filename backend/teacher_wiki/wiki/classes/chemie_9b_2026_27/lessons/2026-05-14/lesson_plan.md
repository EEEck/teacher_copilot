# Lesson Plan - Oxidation Numbers

> Duration: 45 min | Target date: 2026-05-14

## Learning goals
- Assign oxidation numbers to common atoms and ions.
- Distinguish oxidation number from ionic charge.
- Use simple examples with oxygen, hydrogen, chlorine, and metals.

## Lesson flow
- **Warmup** (5 min): Quick review of charge versus oxidation number.
- **Input** (15 min): Build a rules list for common oxidation numbers.
- **Practice** (15 min): Work through mixed examples with simple ions.
- **Close** (10 min): Share answers and identify the tricky cases.

## Warmup
- One board example where charge and oxidation number are not the same.

## Practice
- Assign oxidation numbers to five simple compounds and ions.
- Mark the atom whose oxidation number changes in each example.

## Homework
- Complete the oxidation-number practice sheet.
- Bring one example where the charge rule felt confusing.

## Teacher notes
- Keep the rule list visible during the whole lesson.

## Addresses open loops
- Oxidation-number rules for oxygen, hydrogen, chlorine, and metals
- Charge versus oxidation number

## Addresses misconceptions
- Oxidation number is not the same thing as ionic charge.
- Oxygen is common, but it is not the only route into redox language.
