# LehrplanPLUS Chemie 9 NTG

## Summary
Grade 9 NTG covers five Lernbereiche: scientific methods, atomic structure and
the shortened periodic table, the donor–acceptor / ion and redox concept,
molecular structure and models, and interactions between particles
(structure–property and intermolecular forces), with planned investigations and
explicit competency work.

## Section: c9_methoden — Chemische Erkenntnisgewinnung
Students derive questions from simple everyday phenomena, plan hypothesis-led
experiments, collect and interpret data and communicate evidence in correct
chemical language. They distinguish substance and particle levels, use models
critically, and apply chemical symbol language to atoms, molecules, ions and
simple reactions.

## Section: c9_atombau — Atombau und gekürztes Periodensystem
Students use experimental findings and periodic-table data to describe energy
levels, characterize atoms, determine valence electrons and infer ion charges,
while comparing model representations and their limits.

## Section: c9_ionen_redox — Donator-Akzeptor-Konzept (Ionen und Redox)
Students explain ion formation and electron transfer with the donor–acceptor
concept, including salt formation, electrolysis of solutions or melts, redox
half-equations, and everyday links such as batteries and rechargeable cells.
They connect forced and spontaneous redox processes and interpret metal
behaviour in metal-salt solutions at the particle level.

## Section: c9_molekuele — Moleküle und Struktur-Eigenschafts-Beziehungen
Students use model representations to relate molecular structure, bonding and
properties, and explain the scope and limits of chemical models. They apply a
simple orbital picture, valence formulas, and electron-pair repulsion to
selected molecules, including basic hydrocarbon naming and formula conversions.

## Section: c9_wechselwirkung — Wechselwirkungskonzept (Anziehung zwischen Teilchen)
Students use electronegativity and molecular geometry to judge bond and
molecule polarity, classify selected organic functional groups, and explain
physical properties through intermolecular interactions (including hydrogen
bonding and ion–dipole). They apply the idea to water, solvents, salt
dissolution/hydration, and everyday or environmental examples such as petroleum
products.
